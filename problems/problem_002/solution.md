# Solution

## Method

a) \( y = {G}_{2}{G}_{2}u \)

b) \( y = \left( {{G}_{1} + {G}_{2}}\right) u \)

c) \( y = {GK}\left( {u - {Fy}}\right)  \Rightarrow  \left( {1 + {GKF}}\right) y = {GKu} \Rightarrow  y = \frac{GK}{1 + {GKF}}u \)

d) Let \( x \) be the signal between \( {G}_{1} \) and \( {G}_{2} \) . Then

\[
y = {G}_{2}x = {G}_{2}\frac{{G}_{1}}{1 + {G}_{1}{K}_{1}}\left( {u - {K}_{2}y}\right)
\]

or

\[
\left( {1 + {G}_{1}{K}_{1} + {G}_{2}{G}_{1}{K}_{2}}\right) y = {G}_{2}{G}_{1}u \Rightarrow  y = \frac{{G}_{2}{G}_{1}}{1 + {G}_{1}{K}_{1} + {G}_{2}{G}_{1}{K}_{2}}u
\]

e) \( y = u + {GK}\left( {u - y}\right)  \Rightarrow  \left( {1 + {GK}}\right) y = \left( {1 + {GK}}\right) u \) or \( y = u \) . What is going on here?


## Teaching Points
1. Series connections result in multiplication of transfer functions.
2. Gains can be treated as constant transfer functions.
3. Feedback systems require careful identification of forward and feedback paths.
4. Block-diagram reduction simplifies complex systems into single transfer functions.
