# Problem

## Problem Description
 Show that

\[
{\int }_{-\pi /2}^{\pi /2}{e}^{-R\cos \theta }{d\theta } < \frac{\pi }{R},\;R > 0.
\]

Hint: Use the fact that \( \cos \theta  \geq  1 - 2\left| \theta \right| /\pi \) .


## Subproblems
1. Demonstrate that the integrand $f(\theta) = e^{-R\cos \theta}$ is an even function and simplify the integral limits.
2. Apply the given hint/inequality to establish an upper bound for the integrand on the interval $[0, \pi/2]$.
3. Evaluate the definite integral of the upper bound function analytically.
4. Conclude why the resulting expression is strictly less than $\pi/R$.

## Additional Information
- $R$ is a constant such that $R > 0$.
- The domain of integration is $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$.
- The hint $\cos \theta \geq 1 - 2|\theta|/\pi$ is valid for $\theta \in [-\pi/2, \pi/2]$.

## Constraints
- Show all steps of the integration process.
- Explicitly state the properties of exponents and inequalities used.
- Final result must be compared clearly to the target value $\pi/R$.
