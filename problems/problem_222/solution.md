# Solution

## Method

Root locus plots can be found in Figure G.3. Here we will state if the transfer functions are stable under unit feedback.

a) Open-loop system has poles at \( s = -1 \) and \( s = -10 \). Nyquist plot indicates that no point on the negative real axis is encircled. System is stabilizable for any \( K > 0 \).

b) Open-loop system has poles at \( s = -1 \) and \( s = -10 \) and a zero at \( s = -0.1 \). Nyquist plot indicates that no point on the negative real axis is encircled. System is stabilizable for any \( K > 0 \).

c) Open-loop system has a complex pole-pair at \( s = -0.05 \pm 0.9987j \) and a zero at \( s = -10 \). Nyquist plot indicates that no point on the negative real axis is encircled. System is stabilizable for any \( K > 0 \).

d) Open-loop system has poles at \( s = -10 \) and \( s = +1 \) and a zero at \( s = 0.1 \). Nyquist plot indicates that no point on the negative real axis is encircled. System is not stabilizable for any \( K > 0 \).

e) Open-loop system has a complex pole-pair at \( s = 0.05 \pm 0.9987j \) and a zero at \( s = -10 \). Nyquist plot indicates two counter-clockwise encirclements for any \( K > 0.107 \). System is stabilizable for any \( K > 0.107 \).

f) Open-loop system has poles at \( s = -1 \) and \( s = -10 \) and a zero at \( s = 0 \). Nyquist plot indicates that no point on the negative real axis is encircled. System is stabilizable for any \( K > 0 \).

g) Open-loop system has a double pole at \( s = -1 \) and a zero at \( s = -10 \). Nyquist plot indicates that no point on the negative real axis is encircled. System is stabilizable for any \( K > 0 \).

h) System has poles at \( s = 0 \) and \( s = -10 \) and a zero at \( s = -1 \). Nyquist plot indicates that no point on the negative real axis is encircled. System is stabilizable for any \( K > 0 \).

i) System has a double pole at \( s = 0 \), another pole at \( s = -10 \), and a zero at \( s = -1 \). Nyquist plot indicates that no point on the negative real axis is encircled. System is stabilizable for any \( K > 0 \).

j) System has a double complex pole-pair at \( s = -0.05 \pm 0.9987j \) and a zero at \( s = -10 \). Nyquist plot indicates two clockwise encirclements for any \( K > 8.63 \times 10^{-3} \). System is stabilizable for any \( 0 < K < 8.64 \times 10^{-3} \).

k) System has a double pole at \( s = 0 \), a double pole at \( s = -10 \), and a zero at \( s = -1 \). Nyquist plot indicates one clockwise encirclement for \( K > 1500 \). System is stabilizable for \( K < 1500 \).

## Teaching Points

1. Application of the Nyquist stability criterion to systems with varying pole-zero configurations
2. Interpretation of encirclements and their relation to right-half-plane poles
3. Identification of stabilizable versus non-stabilizable systems
4. Relationship between Nyquist plots and root-locus diagrams
5. Effect of gain variation on closed-loop stability
6. Comparison of frequency-domain and root-locus stability analyses
