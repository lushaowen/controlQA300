# Problem

## Problem Description
Show that the Laplace transform, (3.1), and the inverse Laplace transform, (3.4), are linear operators. Hint: The operator \( L \) is linear when \( L\left( {\alpha {f}_{1} + \beta {f}_{2}}\right)  = {\alpha L}\left( {f}_{1}\right)  + \; {\beta L}\left( {f}_{2}\right) \) for all functions \( {f}_{1},{f}_{2} \) and scalars \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) .

## Subproblems

1. State the definition of a linear operator in the context of functional operators.
2. Apply the definition of the Laplace transform to a linear combination of two functions.
3. Show explicitly that the Laplace transform satisfies the linearity property.
4. Use similar reasoning to argue that the inverse Laplace transform is also linear.
5. Explain why linearity is important for system analysis in the Laplace domain.

## Additional Information

- Assume all functions involved are piecewise continuous and of exponential order.
- Scalars \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) are real (or complex) constants.
- You may use basic properties of definite integrals.

## Constraints

- All steps must be shown clearly.
- Use integral definitions rather than transform tables.
- Mathematical reasoning should be rigorous but concise.
- No reliance on results from other problems.
