# Problem: Heating Dynamics of a Water Heater with Constant Power Input

## Problem Description

The heater is initially filled with water at ambient temperature and then turned on.

The heater is rated at:
- Heating power: \( 40{,}000 \;\mathrm{BTU/h} \approx 12\;\mathrm{kW} \)

Assume:
- Initial water temperature: \( 77^\circ\mathrm{F} \) (approximately \( 25^\circ\mathrm{C} \))
- Desired temperature: \( 140^\circ\mathrm{F} \) (approximately \( 60^\circ\mathrm{C} \))
- Ambient temperature: \( T_o = 25^\circ\mathrm{C} \)
- No water inflow or outflow: \( w = 0 \)

Using the thermal model developed and the thermal resistance identified, calculate the time required to heat the water to the desired temperature.

---

## Subproblems

1. Write the governing thermal differential equation with constant heat input.
2. Solve the differential equation for the temperature response.
3. Determine the equilibrium temperature of the system.
4. Compute the time required for the water temperature to reach \( 60^\circ\mathrm{C} \).
5. Interpret the result in terms of system time constants and heating power.

---

## Additional Information

- Water properties are assumed constant.
- The tank is perfectly mixed.
- Heat losses to the environment are modeled via a thermal resistance.
- All temperatures may be treated in degrees Celsius.

---

## Constraints

- Use SI units consistently.
- Assume constant heating power.
- Neglect nonlinear heat transfer effects.
