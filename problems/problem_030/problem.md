# Problem

## Problem Description

Show that the ordinary differential equation

\[
m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + \left( {{k}_{1} + {k}_{2}}\right) x = 0
\]

is a simplified description of the motion of the mass-spring-damper system in Fig. 2.20(a). What does \( x \) represent? Why do the equations not depend either on the rest lengths \( {\ell }_{0,1},{\ell }_{0,2} \) or on the dimensions \( d \) and \( w \) ? What is the difference between the cases \( d \geq  w + {\ell }_{0,1} + {\ell }_{0,2} \) and \( d \leq  w + {\ell }_{0,1} + {\ell }_{0,2} \) ?

## Subproblems

1. Apply Newton’s second law to the mass.
2. Write expressions for the forces exerted by both springs.
3. Express the total force in terms of displacement \( x \) and reference position \( x_0 \).
4. Determine the value of \( x_0 \) that eliminates constant force terms.
5. Interpret the resulting equation physically.

---

## Additional Information

- Both springs obey Hooke’s law.
- The damper produces a viscous force proportional to velocity.
- Motion is restricted to one dimension.
- All parameters are assumed to be constant and positive.

---

## Constraints

- Use Newton’s second law explicitly.
- Clearly define the meaning of displacement variables.
- Provide both mathematical derivations and physical explanations.
- Do not neglect any forces before choosing the reference position.
