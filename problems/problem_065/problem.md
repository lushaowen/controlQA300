# Problem
## Problem Description

Show that

\[
\mathcal{L}\left\{  \frac{\sin \left( {at}\right) }{t}\right\}   = {\tan }^{-1}\left( \frac{a}{s}\right) .
\]

Hint: Use \( {\int }_{0}^{a}\cos \left( {at}\right) {da} = \sin \left( {at}\right) /t \) .


## Subproblems

1. Use the identity
   \[
   \frac{\sin(at)}{t} = \int_0^{a} \cos(\alpha t)\, d\alpha
   \]
   to rewrite the given function.
2. Apply the definition of the Laplace transform to the rewritten expression.
3. Justify the interchange of the order of integration.
4. Evaluate the resulting Laplace transform using known transform pairs.
5. Compute the remaining integral and simplify the result.

---

## Additional Information

- Assume \( a > 0 \) and \( \Re(s) > 0 \).
- You may use standard Laplace transform tables.
- Absolute convergence may be assumed to justify exchanging integrals.

---

## Constraints

- All intermediate steps must be shown.
- The proof must be analytic and self-contained.
- Clearly state any assumptions used in exchanging limits or integrals.
