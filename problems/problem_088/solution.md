# Solution

We solve each linear ordinary differential equation using the Laplace transform.
Initial conditions are incorporated algebraically in the Laplace domain, and the
solution is obtained by inverse transformation.

---

## Method

### First-Order Equations

**(a)**  
\[
y(t) = e^{-t}
\]

**(b)**  
\[
y(t) = 1 - e^{-t}
\]

**(c)**  
\[
y(t) = e^{t}
\]

**(d)**  
\[
y(t) = e^{t} - 1
\]

---

### Second-Order Homogeneous Equations

**(e)**  
\[
y(t) = e^{-t}
\]

**(g)**  
\[
y(t) = \cos(t) - \sin(t)
\]

**(l)**  
\[
y(t) = e^{-t}(t + 1)
\]

**(n)**  
\[
y(t) = e^{-t}(\sin(t) + \cos(t))
\]

---

### Second-Order Forced Equations

**(f)**  
\[
y(t) = e^{-t}(-t + e^{t} - 1)
\]

**(h)**  
\[
y(t) = \frac{1}{2} t \sin(t)
\]

**(i)**  
\[
y(t) = \frac{5}{3}\sin^2\!\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)(2\cos(t) + 1)
\]

**(j)**  
\[
y(t) = \frac{1}{2} t \sin(t) + \cos(t)
\]

**(k)**  
\[
y(t) = \frac{1}{3}\left(4\cos(t) - \cos(2t)\right)
\]

**(m)**  
\[
y(t) = \frac{1}{2} e^{-t}\left(t - e^{t}\cos(t) + 1\right)
\]

**(o)**  
\[
y(t) = \frac{1}{5} e^{-t}
\Big[(2e^{t}-3)\sin(t) + (e^{t}-1)\cos(t)\Big]
\]

---

## Teaching Points

1. Laplace transforms convert differential equations into algebraic equations
2. Initial conditions are naturally embedded in the Laplace
