# Problem: Closed-Loop Speed Control of a DC Motor Using Proportional Feedback

## Problem Description

A proportional feedback controller is used to regulate the angular speed of a DC motor.  
The control law is given by:

\[
v_a(t) = K\left(\overline{\omega} - \omega(t)\right)
\]

where:
- \( \overline{\omega} \) is the desired angular speed,
- \( \omega(t) \) is the actual motor speed,
- \( K \) is the controller gain.

The DC motor dynamics have previously been identified and are modeled by a first-order differential equation.

Your tasks are to:
1. Derive the closed-loop differential equation describing the motor speed.
2. Solve the resulting closed-loop system.
3. Select a controller gain \( K \) such that the steady-state speed error is less than 10%.
4. Compute the resulting closed-loop time constant.
5. Analyze the motor speed response and the required armature voltage when tracking a reference speed of \( 4000 \) RPM.
6. Determine the maximum armature voltage generated by the controller.

---

## Subproblems

1. Write the closed-loop differential equation using the given control law.
2. Solve the closed-loop system for arbitrary initial conditions.
3. Derive the steady-state speed and steady-state error.
4. Determine the minimum controller gain required to achieve less than 10% steady-state error.
5. Compute the closed-loop time constant.
6. Evaluate the maximum value of the armature voltage \( v_a(t) \).
7. Interpret the physical implications of the controller gain selection.

---

## Additional Information

- Identified DC motor model:
  \[
  \dot{\omega} + \alpha \omega = \beta v_a
  \]
- Model parameters:
  \[
  \alpha = 10\;\mathrm{s^{-1}}, \quad
  \beta \approx 436.3\;\mathrm{rad/(V\,s^2)}
  \]
- Reference speed:
  \[
  \overline{\omega} = 4000\;\mathrm{RPM}
  \]
- Assume zero initial conditions unless otherwise stated.

---

## Constraints

- Assume linear motor dynamics.
- Ignore actuator saturation unless explicitly stated.
- All derivations must be shown.
- Use SI units for analysis.
