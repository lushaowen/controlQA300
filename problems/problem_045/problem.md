# Problem: Gravity-Induced Forcing in a Mass-Spring-Damper System

## Problem Description
 Show that the ordinary differential equation

\[
m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + {kx} = {mg}\sin \theta
\]

is a simplified description of the motion of the mass-spring-damper system in Fig. 2.19(b), where \( g \) is the gravitational acceleration and \( {x}_{0} \) is equal to the spring rest length \( {\ell }_{0} \) .
![](images\bo_d5cu23n7aajc7381m4sg_5_316_1016_953_468_0.jpg)
Figure 2.19 
## Subproblems

1. Identify all forces acting on the mass along the direction of motion.
2. Apply Newton’s second law to write the force balance equation.
3. Express the spring force when the displacement is measured from a general reference position \( x_0 \).
4. Show explicitly how choosing \( x_0 = \ell_0 \) simplifies the equation.
5. Interpret the physical meaning of the gravitational term \( mg\sin\theta \).

---

## Additional Information

- The spring obeys Hooke’s law.
- The damper produces a force proportional to velocity.
- Motion is constrained to a single direction along the incline.
- Gravitational acceleration is constant.

---

## Constraints

- Use Newton’s second law explicitly.
- Clearly define displacement variables and reference positions.
- Assume linear spring and viscous damping behavior.
- Provide both mathematical derivation and physical explanation.
