# Problem: Vertical Position of a Falling Object with Air Resistance

## Problem Description
Calculate the vertical position, \( x\left( t\right) \) , corresponding to the velocity, \( v\left( t\right) \) , computed in P2.5. How does the vertical position, \( x\left( t\right) \) , relate to the height measured from the ground, \( h\left( t\right) \) , of a free-falling object? Use the same data as in P2.5 and sketch or use MATLAB to plot the position \( x\left( t\right) \) and the height \( h\left( t\right) \) , when \( x\left( 0\right)  = 0, h\left( 0\right)  = 1 \) , and \( v\left( 0\right)  = 0, v\left( 0\right)  = 1\mathrm{\;m}/\mathrm{s} \) , or \( v\left( 0\right)  =  - 1\mathrm{\;m}/\mathrm{s} \) for \( 1\mathrm{\;s} \) .


## Subproblems
1. Compute the position $x(t)$ by integrating the velocity response from P2.5.
2. Express the height $h(t)$ in terms of the position $x(t)$.
3. Analyze the effect of different initial velocities on $x(t)$ and $h(t)$.
4. Interpret the physical meaning of $x(t)$ and $h(t)$.

## Assumptions
- Motion is vertical and one-dimensional.
- Linear air resistance.
- Constant gravitational acceleration.
- Same model and parameters as in P2.5.

## Constraints
- Use analytical integration.
- Clearly distinguish between position and height.
- Results must be consistent with P2.5.
