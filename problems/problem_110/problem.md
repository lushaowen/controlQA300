# Problem

## Problem Description
Consider the simplified model of the elevator described. Calculate the elevator’s linear position, \( {x}_{1}\left( t\right) \) , obtained in response to a constant-torque input, \( \tau \left( t\right)  = \; \widetilde{\tau }, t \geq  0 \) , and a constant gravitational torque \( w\left( t\right)  = \operatorname{gr}\left( {{m}_{1} - {m}_{2}}\right) , t \geq  0 \) . Identify the transient and the steady-state components of the response.

## Subproblems
1. Express the constant motor torque and gravitational torque in the Laplace domain.
2. Recall the transfer function relating torque inputs to elevator velocity from P3.62.
3. Derive the Laplace-domain expression for the elevator’s linear position.
4. Perform partial fraction expansion of the resulting expression.
5. Compute the inverse Laplace transform to obtain \( x_1(t) \).
6. Identify the transient component of the position response.
7. Identify the steady-state component of the position response.
8. Interpret the physical meaning of the steady-state term.

## Additional Information
- The elevator system is linear and time-invariant.
- Zero initial conditions are assumed.
- The position is obtained by integrating the velocity response.
- Parameters \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) are defined as in P3.62.
- Standard Laplace transform tables and properties may be used.

## Constraints
- Use Laplace-domain analysis consistently.
- Clearly distinguish between transient and steady-state terms.
- Assume all system parameters are positive.
- Final expressions should be simplified and physically meaningful.
