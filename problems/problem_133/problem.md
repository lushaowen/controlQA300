# Problem

## Problem Description

Show that if \( G \) and \( K \) are rational transfer-functions and there are no pole-zero cancellations when forming the product \( GK \) then the zeros of \( \widehat{K} \), are the poles of \( G \).

## Subproblems

1. Recall the definition of \( \widehat{K} \) given.
2. Identify the poles of the modified plant \( \widetilde{G} \).
3. Analyze the pole-zero structure of the product \( \widetilde{G}K \).
4. Determine how poles of a transfer-function relate to zeros of its inverse.
5. Use these relationships to identify the zeros of \( \widehat{K} \).
6. Conclude why these zeros correspond to the poles of \( G \).

## Additional Information

- All transfer-functions are assumed to be rational.
- No pole-zero cancellations occur when forming the product \( GK \).
- Standard properties of poles and zeros in feedback systems apply.
- The notation \( \widetilde{G} \) and \( \widehat{K} \) follows that of P4.11.

## Constraints

- The argument should rely on pole-zero analysis only.
- No numerical examples are required.
- Assumptions regarding cancellations must be explicitly used.
- The reasoning should remain within linear systems theory.
