# Problem: Heating Dynamics of a Water Heater with Constant Power and Flow

## Problem Description

Repeat the heating analysis performed, but now assume that a constant inflow and outflow of water at ambient temperature is present.

The water heater has the following characteristics:
- Heating power: \( 40{,}000\;\mathrm{BTU/h} \approx 12\;\mathrm{kW} \)
- Volumetric flow rate: \( 20\;\mathrm{gal/h} \approx 21 \times 10^{-6}\;\mathrm{m^3/s} \)
- Inflow and ambient temperature: \( 77^\circ\mathrm{F} \approx 25^\circ\mathrm{C} \)
- Initial water temperature: \( 25^\circ\mathrm{C} \)
- Desired temperature: \( 140^\circ\mathrm{F} \approx 60^\circ\mathrm{C} \)

Using the thermal resistance identified, determine how the presence of water flow affects the heating dynamics.

---

## Subproblems

1. Write the governing thermal differential equation including heat input and flow.
2. Solve the differential equation for the temperature response.
3. Compute the equilibrium temperature with flow.
4. Calculate the time required to reach \( 60^\circ\mathrm{C} \).
5. Compare the result with the no-flow case in P2.52 and explain the difference.

---

## Additional Information

- Use the lumped thermal model from P2.49.
- Assume perfect mixing of water in the tank.
- Heat input and flow rate are constant.
- Water properties are unchanged from P2.50.

---

## Constraints

- Use SI units consistently.
- Neglect nonlinear heat-transfer effects.
- Clearly distinguish transient and steady-state behavior.
