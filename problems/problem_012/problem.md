# Problem

## Problem Description
A sky diver weighing \( {70}\mathrm{\;{kg}} \) reaches a constant vertical speed of \( {200}\mathrm{\;{km}}/\mathrm{h} \) during the free-fall phase of the dive and a vertical speed of \( {20}\mathrm{\;{km}}/\mathrm{h} \) after the parachute is opened. Approximate each phase of the fall by the ordinary differential equation obtained in P2.4 and estimate the resistance coefficients using the given information. Use \( g = {10}\mathrm{\;m}/{\mathrm{s}}^{2} \) . What are the time-constants in each phase? At what time and distance from the ground should the parachute be opened if the landing speed is to be less than or equal to \( {29}\mathrm{\;{km}}/\mathrm{h} \) ? If a dive starts at a height of \( 4\mathrm{\;{km}} \) with zero vertical velocity at the moment of the jump and the parachute is opened \( {60}\mathrm{\;s} \) into the dive, how long is the diver airborne?
## Subproblems
1. Compute the terminal velocities and corresponding resistance coefficients.
2. Compute the time constants for each phase.
3. Analyze the transient response after the parachute opens.
4. Estimate the minimum safe opening height.
5. Compute the total fall time.

## Assumptions
- Motion is vertical and one-dimensional.
- Linear air resistance model applies in each phase.
- Parameters are piecewise constant.
- Transition between phases is instantaneous.

## Constraints
- Use the model from P2.4.
- Clearly separate free-fall and parachute phases.
- Use consistent units throughout.
