# Problem

## Problem Description

Consider the satellite model. Letting \( M \approx  6 \times  {10}^{24}\mathrm{\;{kg}} \) be the mass of the earth, and \( G \approx  {6.7} \times  {10}^{-{11}}\mathrm{\;N}{\mathrm{\;m}}^{2}/{\mathrm{{kg}}}^{2} \) , calculate the altitude, \( R \) , of a 1600 kg GPS satellite in medium earth orbit (MEO) with a period of 11 h. If earth's radius is approximately \( 6 \times  {10}^{3}\mathrm{\;{km}} \) , calculate the satellite’s altitude measured from the surface of the earth. Is the GPS satellite equilibrium point asymptotically stable? Is it unstable? Does it depend on the mass of the satellite?

## Subproblems

1. Convert the orbital period into angular velocity.
2. Use gravitational equilibrium conditions to compute the orbital radius.
3. Determine the satellite altitude relative to the earth’s surface.
4. Analyze the stability of the equilibrium using the linearized orbital model.
5. Classify the equilibrium as asymptotically stable, unstable, or marginally stable.
6. Determine whether stability depends on satellite mass.
7. Interpret the physical meaning of the stability result.

## Additional Information

- The satellite is assumed to be in a circular orbit.
- Gravitational interaction follows Newton’s law of gravitation.
- Linearized dynamics are based on models derived in P5.41 and P5.42.
- Stability is assessed using eigenvalue analysis of the linearized system.
- Earth’s mass is assumed constant and dominant.

## Constraints

- Use SI units consistently.
- Analytical reasoning must align with the linearized model assumptions.
- Stability conclusions must be supported by eigenvalue analysis.
- Ignore atmospheric drag and perturbations.
