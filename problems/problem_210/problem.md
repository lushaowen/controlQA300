# Problem

## Problem Description

Consider the one-eighth-car model from P6.19. Calculate the transfer-function from the road profile, \( y \), to the mass relative displacement, \( z \). Calculate the value of the spring stiffness, \( k \), and shock absorber damping coefficient, \( b \), for a car with \( 1/4 \) mass equal to \( {640}\mathrm{\;{kg}} \) to have a natural frequency \( {f}_{\mathrm{n}} = {2.5}\mathrm{\;{Hz}} \) and damping ratio \( \zeta = {0.08} \). Locate the roots in the complex plane.

## Subproblems

1. Write the equation of motion for the one-eighth-car suspension model.
2. Define the relative displacement \( z \) in terms of mass and road motion.
3. Derive the transfer function from the road input \( y \) to the output \( z \).
4. Express the characteristic equation in standard second-order form.
5. Compute the spring stiffness required to achieve the desired natural frequency.
6. Compute the damping coefficient corresponding to the given damping ratio.
7. Determine and interpret the locations of the system poles in the complex plane.

## Additional Information

- The suspension is modeled as a linear mass-spring-damper system.
- Road displacement is treated as a base excitation.
- Only vertical dynamics are considered.
- Gravitational effects are neglected in the dynamic equation.

## Constraints

- The system is assumed to be linear and time-invariant.
- Small displacement assumptions apply.
- Parameters \( k \) and \( b \) must be physically realizable.
- Frequency-domain analysis is required.
