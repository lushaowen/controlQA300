# Problem

## Problem Description

Show that \( {K}_{\mathrm{t}} = {K}_{\mathrm{e}} \). Hint: Equate the mechanical power with the electric power.

## Subproblems

1. Identify the physical meaning of the torque constant \( K_t \) and the back-emf constant \( K_e \).
2. Write the expression for mechanical power generated by the motor shaft.
3. Write the expression for electrical power associated with the back electromotive force.
4. Equate the mechanical and electrical power expressions under ideal energy conversion assumptions.
5. Deduce the relationship between \( K_t \) and \( K_e \).

## Additional Information

- The system under consideration is an ideal DC motor.
- Losses due to friction, resistance, and magnetic effects are neglected.
- The armature current is denoted by \( i_a \), torque by \( \tau \), angular velocity by \( \omega \), and back-emf voltage by \( v_e \).
- The derivation assumes steady-state or instantaneous power balance.

## Constraints

- Assume ideal electromechanical energy conversion.
- Use power balance arguments only; no numerical substitution.
- Clearly distinguish between mechanical and electrical quantities.
- All symbols must be interpreted using standard DC motor conventions.
