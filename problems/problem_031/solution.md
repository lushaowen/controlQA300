# Solution

## Method
With Newton's second law help we write

\[
{m}_{1}{\ddot{x}}_{1} =  - {b}_{1}{\dot{x}}_{1} + {b}_{2}\left( {{\dot{x}}_{2} - {\dot{x}}_{1}}\right)  - {k}_{1}{x}_{1} + {k}_{2}\left( {{x}_{2} - {x}_{1}}\right)
\]

\[
=  - \left( {{b}_{1} + {b}_{2}}\right) {\dot{x}}_{1} - \left( {{k}_{1} + {k}_{2}}\right) {x}_{1} - {b}_{2}{\dot{x}}_{2} - {k}_{2}{x}_{2}
\]

and

\[
{m}_{2}{\ddot{x}}_{2} =  - {b}_{2}\left( {{\dot{x}}_{2} - {\dot{x}}_{1}}\right)  - {k}_{2}\left( {{x}_{2} - {x}_{1}}\right)  + {f}_{2}
\]

after choosing \( {x}_{1} \) and \( {x}_{2} \) as displacements from equilibrium.


## Teaching Points

1. Multi-mass systems naturally lead to coupled differential equations
2. Relative displacement and velocity describe interaction forces
3. Choosing equilibrium-based coordinates simplifies modeling
4. Coupling elements transmit forces between subsystems
5. External inputs may affect only part of a coupled system
