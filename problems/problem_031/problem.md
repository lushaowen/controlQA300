# Problem: Coupled Equations of Motion in a Two-Mass Spring-Damper System

## Problem Description
Show that the ordinary differential equations

\[
{m}_{1}{\ddot{x}}_{1} + \left( {{b}_{1} + {b}_{2}}\right) {\dot{x}}_{1} + \left( {{k}_{1} + {k}_{2}}\right) {x}_{1} - {b}_{2}{\dot{x}}_{2} - {k}_{2}{x}_{2} = 0,
\]

\[
{m}_{2}{\ddot{x}}_{2} + {b}_{2}\left( {{\dot{x}}_{2} - {\dot{x}}_{1}}\right)  + {k}_{2}\left( {{x}_{2} - {x}_{1}}\right)  = {f}_{2}
\]

constitute a simplified description of the motion of the mass-spring-damper system in Fig. 2.20(b), where \( {x}_{1} = {x}_{2} = 0 \) when the length of both springs is equal to their rest lengths and \( {f}_{2} \) is a force applied on mass \( {m}_{2} \) .

## Subproblems

1. Identify all forces acting on mass \( m_1 \).
2. Apply Newton’s second law to derive the equation of motion for \( m_1 \).
3. Identify all forces acting on mass \( m_2 \).
4. Apply Newton’s second law to derive the equation of motion for \( m_2 \).
5. Explain the physical meaning of the coupling terms involving \( x_2 - x_1 \) and \( \dot{x}_2 - \dot{x}_1 \).
6. Explain why choosing \( x_1 \) and \( x_2 \) as displacements from equilibrium simplifies the equations.

---

## Additional Information

- All springs obey Hooke’s law.
- All dampers are linear viscous dampers.
- Motion is restricted to one dimension.
- The system is linear and time-invariant.

---

## Constraints

- Use Newton’s second law explicitly for each mass.
- Clearly indicate interaction forces between the two masses.
- Do not omit coupling terms during derivation.
- Provide physical interpretation in addition to mathematical results.
