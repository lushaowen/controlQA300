# Problem: Partial Fraction Expansion of Rational Functions

## Problem Description

Compute the partial fraction expansion of the following rational functions of the complex variable $s$.
These expressions frequently appear in Laplace transform inversion and linear system analysis.

For improper rational functions, perform polynomial division first and then decompose the remaining
proper rational part.

---

## Subproblems

Compute the partial fraction expansion of:

### Distinct Linear Factors
(a) $\displaystyle \frac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)}$  
(b) $\displaystyle \frac{s-1}{(s+1)(s+2)}$  
(c) $\displaystyle \frac{s(s-1)}{(s+1)(s+2)}$  
(d) $\displaystyle \frac{s^2-1}{(s+1)(s+2)}$  

### Improper Rational Functions
(e) $\displaystyle \frac{s^2(s-1)}{(s+1)(s+2)}$  
(f) $\displaystyle \frac{1}{s(s+1)(s+2)}$  

### Repeated Poles
(g) $\displaystyle \frac{1}{s^2(s+1)}$  
(h) $\displaystyle \frac{s-1}{s^2(s+1)}$  

### Quadratic Factors
(i) $\displaystyle \frac{1}{s^2+2s+1}$  
(j) $\displaystyle \frac{s}{s^2+2s+1}$  
(k) $\displaystyle \frac{1}{s^2+2s+2}$  
(l) $\displaystyle \frac{s+1}{(s^2+2s+2)^2}$  

---

## Additional Information

- Polynomial division is required if the numerator degree is not less than the denominator degree.
- Quadratic factors may represent repeated real poles or complex conjugate poles.
- Complex roots may be expressed using $j=\sqrt{-1}$.
- Any algebraically equivalent partial fraction form is acceptable.

---

## Constraints

- Final expressions must be fully decomposed.
- Clearly separate polynomial and proper rational parts.
- No inverse Laplace transforms are required.
