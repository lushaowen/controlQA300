# Problem

## Problem Description
Show that the ordinary differential equations

\[
{m}_{1}{\ddot{x}}_{1} + b\left( {{\dot{x}}_{1} - {\dot{x}}_{2}}\right)  + k\left( {{x}_{1} - {x}_{2}}\right)  = 0,
\]

\[
{m}_{2}{\ddot{x}}_{2} + b\left( {{\dot{x}}_{2} - {\dot{x}}_{1}}\right)  + k\left( {{x}_{2} - {x}_{1}}\right)  = 0,
\]

constitute a simplified description of the motion of the mass-spring-damper system in Fig. 2.21 where \( {x}_{1} = {x}_{2} = 0 \) when the length of the spring is its rest length. Show that it is possible to decouple the equations if you write them in the coordinates

\[
{y}_{1} = \frac{{m}_{1}{x}_{1} + {m}_{2}{x}_{2}}{{m}_{1} + {m}_{2}},\;{y}_{2} = {x}_{1} - {x}_{2}.
\]

Use what you know from physics to explain why.

## Subproblems

1. Apply Newton’s second law to each mass.
2. Identify the interaction forces between the two masses.
3. Express the equations of motion in matrix or coupled form.
4. Perform a coordinate transformation to \( y_1 \) and \( y_2 \).
5. Derive the resulting equations of motion in the new coordinates.
6. Interpret the physical meaning of each decoupled equation.

---

## Additional Information

- The spring and damper act only on the relative displacement and velocity.
- No external forces act on the system.
- Motion is restricted to one dimension.
- The system is linear and symmetric.

---

## Constraints

- Use Newton’s second law explicitly.
- Clearly justify each algebraic manipulation.
- Maintain consistency in the definition of transformed coordinates.
- Provide both mathematical derivation and physical explanation.
