# Problem: Estimation of Thermal Resistance of a Water Heater

## Problem Description

A water heater is modeled as a lumped thermal system using the differential equation derived in P2.49.
The heater is initially full of water and then left unused for several days.

Assume the following properties of water:
- Density: \( 997.1\;\mathrm{kg/m^3} \)
- Specific heat: \( c = 4186\;\mathrm{J/(kg\cdot K)} \)

A 50-gallon water heater (approximately \( 0.19\;\mathrm{m^3} \)) is initially at a temperature of
\( 140^\circ\mathrm{F} \) (approximately \( 60^\circ\mathrm{C} \)).

The heater is turned off and left:
- With no heat input: \( q = 0 \)
- With no water flow: \( w = 0 \)
- At a constant ambient temperature of \( 77^\circ\mathrm{F} \) (approximately \( 25^\circ\mathrm{C} \))

After 7 days, the water temperature is measured to be approximately
\( 80^\circ\mathrm{F} \) (approximately \( 27^\circ\mathrm{C} \)).

---

## Subproblems

1. Simplify the temperature dynamics under the assumptions \( q = 0 \) and \( w = 0 \).
2. Solve the resulting differential equation.
3. Use the measured temperature after 7 days to estimate the thermal resistance \( R \).
4. Interpret the physical meaning of the estimated thermal resistance.
5. Comment on the realism of the result.

---

## Additional Information

- Assume perfect mixing inside the water heater.
- Neglect heat losses other than those captured by the thermal resistance \( R \).
- Use SI units consistently.
- Time is measured in seconds.

---

## Constraints

- Use the lumped-parameter thermal model from P2.49.
- All parameters are constant.
- Show all intermediate calculation steps.
