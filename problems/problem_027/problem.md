# Problem

## Problem Description
Choose \( {x}_{0} \) wisely to show that the ordinary differential equation

\[
m\ddot{x} + b\dot{x} + {kx} = f
\]

is a simplified description of the motion of the mass-spring-damper system in Fig. 2.19(a), where \( f \) is a force applied on mass \( m \) . Why does the equation not depend on the spring rest length \( {\ell }_{0} \) ?

![](images\bo_d5cu23n7aajc7381m4sg_5_316_1016_953_468_0.jpg)
## Subproblems

1. Apply Newton’s second law to the mass and write the force balance equation.
2. Identify all forces acting on the mass, including spring and damping forces.
3. Define the displacement variable \( x \) with respect to a reference position \( x_0 \).
4. Show how choosing \( x_0 = \ell_0 \) simplifies the equation of motion.
5. Explain why the spring rest length \( \ell_0 \) does not appear in the final equation.

---

## Additional Information

- The spring force is proportional to the extension or compression from its rest length.
- The damper force is proportional to the velocity of the mass.
- The system is assumed to move in one dimension.
- Gravity effects are either neglected or absorbed into the equilibrium position.

---

## Constraints

- Use Newton’s second law explicitly.
- Clearly define all variables and reference points.
- Assume linear spring and linear viscous damping.
- Provide physical interpretation, not just mathematical manipulation.
