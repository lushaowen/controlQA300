# Problem

## Problem Description
Consider the simplified model of the elevator described. Calculate the elevator’s linear velocity, \( {v}_{1}\left( t\right) \) , obtained in response to a constant-torque input, \( \tau \left( t\right)  = \; \widetilde{\tau }, t \geq  0 \) , and a constant gravitational torque \( w\left( t\right)  = \operatorname{gr}\left( {{m}_{1} - {m}_{2}}\right) , t \geq  0 \) . Identify the transient and the steady-state components of the response.

## Subproblems
1. Express the constant motor torque and gravitational torque in the Laplace domain.
2. Recall the transfer functions from P3.62 relating torque inputs to elevator velocity.
3. Compute the Laplace-domain expression for the elevator’s linear velocity.
4. Perform partial fraction expansion of the resulting expression.
5. Compute the inverse Laplace transform to obtain \( v_1(t) \).
6. Identify the transient component of the velocity response.
7. Identify the steady-state component of the velocity response.

## Additional Information
- The elevator system is modeled as linear and time-invariant.
- Zero initial conditions are assumed.
- Both the motor torque and gravitational torque are constant step inputs.
- The system parameters \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) are defined as in P3.62.
- Standard Laplace transform properties and tables may be used.

## Constraints
- Use Laplace-domain analysis throughout the solution.
- Clearly separate transient and steady-state terms.
- Assume all physical parameters are positive constants.
- Final expressions should be simplified and physically interpretable.
