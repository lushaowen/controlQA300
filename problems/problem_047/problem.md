# Problem: DC Motor Speed Control with Actuator Voltage Constraint

## Problem Description

A proportional feedback controller is used to regulate the angular speed of a DC motor:

\[
v_a(t) = K\left(\overline{\omega} - \omega(t)\right)
\]

where:
- \( \overline{\omega} \) is the desired angular speed,
- \( \omega(t) \) is the actual motor speed,
- \( K \) is the controller gain.

In previous analysis, the controller gain was selected to achieve a small steady-state error, which resulted in a large initial control voltage.
In this problem, redesign the controller gain such that the **maximum armature voltage** generated by the controller **never exceeds 12 V** when tracking a reference speed of \( \overline{\omega} = 4000 \) RPM.

---

## Subproblems

1. Determine the relationship between the controller gain \( K \) and the initial armature voltage \( v_a(0) \).
2. Compute the maximum allowable controller gain given the voltage constraint.
3. Calculate the resulting closed-loop time constant.
4. Describe the qualitative effect of the voltage constraint on the system response.
5. Compare this design with the previous unconstrained controller.

---

## Additional Information

- Identified DC motor model:
  \[
  \dot{\omega} + \alpha \omega = \beta v_a
  \]
- Model parameters:
  \[
  \alpha = 10\;\mathrm{s^{-1}}, \quad
  \beta \approx 436.3\;\mathrm{rad/(V\,s^2)}
  \]
- Reference speed:
  \[
  \overline{\omega} = 4000\;\mathrm{RPM}
  \]
- Assume zero initial speed:
  \[
  \omega(0) = 0
  \]

---

## Constraints

- The armature voltage must satisfy:
  \[
  |v_a(t)| \le 12\;\mathrm{V}
  \]
- Assume linear motor behavior.
- Ignore actuator saturation dynamics.
- Use SI units throughout.
