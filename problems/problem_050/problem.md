# Problem: Effect of Flow Rate on Cooling Time of a Water Heater

## Problem Description

Consider the same water heater and thermal conditions described.
The heater is initially full of water at a high temperature and then allowed to cool down.

This time, however, a constant inflow and outflow of water at ambient temperature is present.

Assume:
- Initial water temperature: \( 140^\circ\mathrm{F} \) (approximately \( 60^\circ\mathrm{C} \))
- Ambient and inflow temperature: \( 77^\circ\mathrm{F} \) (approximately \( 25^\circ\mathrm{C} \))
- Desired final temperature: \( 80^\circ\mathrm{F} \) (approximately \( 27^\circ\mathrm{C} \))
- Constant volumetric flow rate: \( 20\;\mathrm{gal/h} \approx 21 \times 10^{-6}\;\mathrm{m^3/s} \)



## Subproblems

1. Write the thermal model including the effect of constant water flow.
2. Solve the resulting differential equation for temperature.
3. Compute the time required for the water temperature to reach \( 27^\circ\mathrm{C} \).
4. Compare this cooling time with the case when no water flows through the heater.
5. Explain physically why the time constants differ so dramatically.

---

## Additional Information

- Use the lumped-parameter thermal model from P2.49.
- Assume perfect mixing inside the tank.
- Heat input is zero: \( q = 0 \).
- Inflow and outflow temperatures are equal to ambient temperature.

---

## Constraints

- All parameters are constant.
- Use SI units consistently.
- Show all intermediate steps.
