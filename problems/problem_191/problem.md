# Problem

## Problem Description

The exponential model

\[
\dot{x} = rx
\]

has been used by Malthus to study population growth. In this context, \( x \) is the current population and \( r = b - m \) is the growth rate, where \( b \) is the birth rate and \( m \) is the mortality rate.

Explain the behavior of this model when \( b > m \), \( b < m \), and \( b = m \).

## Subproblems

1. Solve the differential equation \( \dot{x} = rx \) explicitly.
2. Relate the sign of the growth rate \( r \) to population behavior.
3. Interpret the case \( b > m \) in terms of long-term population trends.
4. Interpret the case \( b < m \) in terms of extinction or decay.
5. Explain the equilibrium behavior when \( b = m \).

## Additional Information

- The model assumes unlimited resources and no environmental constraints.
- The growth rate \( r \) is constant over time.
- Initial population \( x(0) \) is assumed to be nonnegative.
- The model is deterministic and continuous-time.

## Constraints

- Explanations should be qualitative and based on the analytical solution.
- No additional nonlinear effects should be introduced.
- Interpretations must be consistent with the mathematical model.
