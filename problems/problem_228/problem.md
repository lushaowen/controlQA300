# Problem

## Problem Description

you designed the spring and damper on the one-quarter-car model  for a car with \( 1/4 \) mass \( m_{\mathrm{s}} = 600\,\mathrm{kg} \), wheel mass \( m_{\mathrm{u}} = 40\,\mathrm{kg} \), tire stiffness equal to \( k_{\mathrm{u}} = 200{,}000\,\mathrm{N/m} \), and negligible tire damping coefficient \( b_{\mathrm{u}} = 0 \), to have its dominant poles display a natural frequency \( f_{\mathrm{n}} = 2.5\,\mathrm{Hz} \) and damping ratio \( \zeta = 0.08 \). Interpret your result using Bode plots and the frequency-response method.

## Subproblems

1. Write the state-space model of the one-quarter-car system.
2. Identify the output variable represented by the transfer function.
3. Derive the transfer function corresponding to the designed suspension.
4. Determine the pole locations and identify the dominant pole pair.
5. Draw or compute the Bode magnitude and phase plots.
6. Locate the resonant frequency from the Bode magnitude plot.
7. Relate the resonant frequency to the natural frequency and damping ratio.
8. Interpret the frequency-response characteristics in terms of vehicle ride dynamics.

## Additional Information

- The one-quarter-car model captures both sprung and unsprung mass dynamics.
- Tire damping is neglected to emphasize suspension dynamics.
- The output corresponds to sprung-mass acceleration.
- Frequency-domain analysis is used to interpret resonance and vibration amplification.

## Constraints

- Use frequency-response and Bode plot methods explicitly.
- Interpret results both mathematically and physically.
- Numerical values must be consistent with P6.25.
- The dominant dynamics should be clearly identified.
