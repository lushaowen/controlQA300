# Problem

## Problem Description

Consider the simplified model of the thermodynamic system described. Calculate the substance’s temperature, \( T(t) \), obtained in response to a constant ambient temperature,
\( T_{\mathrm{o}}(t) = \widetilde{T}_{\mathrm{o}},\ t \geq 0 \),
a constant inflow temperature,
\( T_{\mathrm{i}}(t) = \widetilde{T}_{\mathrm{i}},\ t \geq 0 \),
and a constant heat source
\( q(t) = \widetilde{q},\ t \geq 0 \).
Identify the transient and the steady-state components of the response.

## Subproblems

1. Write the Laplace-domain expression for the system temperature \( T(s) \) using superposition.
2. Substitute the Laplace transforms of the constant inputs.
3. Combine the individual input contributions into a single expression.
4. Perform partial fraction expansion of \( T(s) \).
5. Compute the inverse Laplace transform to obtain \( T(t) \).
6. Identify the transient component of the temperature response.
7. Identify the steady-state component of the temperature response.

## Additional Information

- The system is linear and time-invariant.
- Zero initial conditions are assumed.
- Parameters \( \alpha \) and \( \beta \) are positive constants defined by the system model.
- Superposition applies due to linearity.

## Constraints

- Laplace transform methods must be used.
- Partial fraction expansion must be explicitly shown.
- Transient and steady-state components must be clearly distinguished.
- Final expressions should be physically interpretable.
